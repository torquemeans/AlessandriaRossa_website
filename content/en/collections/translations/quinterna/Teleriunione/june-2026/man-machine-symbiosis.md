Tuesday’s online meeting began with a comment on recent developments in the field of Artificial Intelligence (AI). 

Nello Cristianini’s last essay, *Forma mentis. La corsa per decifrare i pensieri delle macchine*[^1] and Dario Amodei’s “[The Urgency of interpretability](https://darioamodei.com/post/the-urgency-of-interpretability)” were brought up. AI experts are still trying to comprehend the inner workings of the so called *black box*, which is to say, that set of still mostly unknown processes which allow the functioning of *Large Language Models* (LLMs). While the inputs (data, information) and outputs (elaborated information) are decipherable, it is still mostly unclear how these systems reach their conclusions.

A report published on Anthropic’s website, called “[When AI builds itself](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement)”, talks about the significant progress that has been made in the field of recursive self-improvement in recent years. There has been a shift from entirely human-written code to the usage of systems such as Claude Code, which today generates 80% of the code used by Anthropic (*The Economist*, “[How artificial intelligence got better at building itself](https://www.economist.com/science-and-technology/2026/06/07/how-artificial-intelligence-got-better-at-building-itself))”. The inflection point hasn’t yet been reached: we are still in a phase of exponential growth and, according to multiple researchers, in the next years we will witness profound technological, and thus, [social](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/40/verso_la_singolarita_storica.htm), changes.

For the bourgeoisie, the urgency of interpretability resides in the fact that no business or State can allow itself to ignore the evolution of these systems, as the risks linked to their growing autonomy manifest themselves more and more. For example, in the military industry, AI systems such as Lavender and Gospel “suggest” who or what to target; in the field of genetics, AI contributes to the development of new therapies and enhances the precision of genetic interventions, but might also foster the design and spread of highly dangerous biological weapons.

In a *YouTube* interview (“[AI doesn’t just repeat, it truly understands](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IxsV2lFLVUw)”), Nello Cristianini claims that the currently most widespread opinion, according to which AI systems can learn only through statistical correlations, is reductive: the most recent models are, in fact, beginning to operate on abstract concepts. Just like Marx, during the writing of Volume I of Capital, dedicated himself to the study of the [inner workings of the factory](https://www.quinterna.org/archivio/filitempo/110_1953_animacavallo.htm), it is necessary to analyse AI’s mechanisms to understand its scope. Artificial neural networks are at the root of these systems. An emblematic case is that of AlphaZero, developed by Google DeepMind, which has revolutionized the game of chess: beginning from the sole knowledge of its base rules, the program learned chess through millions of games against itself, reaching a “superhuman” performance.

Research on AI models has shown how specific artificial neural structures tend to activate themselves after certain words, concepts or images, in a way that is, in some respects, analogous to what happens in the neural circuits of the biological brain. Although LLMs are based on statistical models, they have demonstrated capabilities that go from beyond the simple probabilistic elaboration of data, to improving from past mistakes, adapting, and learning.

The [man-machine](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/38/dalla_necessita_alla_liberta.htm) relation tends to become even more symbiotic (Joël de Rosnay, Kevin Kelly). Anthropic's engineers claim that LLMs are developing a kind of "forma mentis", not explicitly designed by anyone, but a result of the interaction and cooperation over the years of thousands of subjects across companies, universities, and research communities. For humans, it is essential to know how machines arrive at their elaborations, as this could constitute the key to understanding how humans themselves understand. In the academic world today, a multidisciplinary approach prevails that perpetuates the separation of the different spheres of knowledge, reflecting the social division of labour. Something else will be needed, namely, a [unified theory of knowledge](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/16/16_rivista.htm) (*n*+1, issues 15–16).

Anthropic has launched a global call for a [moratorium](https://www.lastampa.it/economia/2026/06/05/news/anthropic_limite_aggiornamento_intelligenza_artificiale_perdita_controllo-15648588/) on AI development, and the Church calls for sobriety in the use of these tools and for the defense of ethics centred on the needs of the person (the *[Magnifica Humanitas](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/it/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html)*[^2] encyclical by Pope Leo XIV). The fact remains that, under capitalism, every company and every State, in order to protect its own interests, cannot stop the race for primacy in technological development. The large monopolistic groups behind the development of these technologies (Google/Gemini, Microsoft/Copilot, OpenAI/ChatGPT, DeepSeek, Anthropic/Claude) are dragged by a competitive dynamic that allows no pauses or interruptions.

Today there is much talk about the problem of controlling AI, often evoking scenarios like The Matrix or Terminator. In reality, most human activities are already out of control. In a capitalism that has reached its senile phase, beyond bourgeois propaganda, there is no control over the economy, wars, or social life. There is, at best, a more or less effective management of disorder.

Anthropic will debut on the stock market by the end of the year, but OpenAI is just as ready to go public in September (SpaceX will debut in June). Underneath the enormous value that will catalyse around these *Big Tech* companies, there are no "solid foundations", these companies having relatively few employees, producing systems that contribute to the mass elimination of human labour. The first version of an AI model produces the second, faster and more performant, the second generates a third, better than the previous one, and so on. Much like what happens in the [evolution of life](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/58/le-determinazioni-materiali-e-l-ambiente.htm), from the simplest system to the most complex one. Engineer Daniel Hillis, a few years ago, regarding the development of autonomous machines, wrote:

*“We're beginning to depend on computers that use a process very different from engineering—a process that allows us to produce things with much more complexity than we could with normal engineering. Yet we don't quite understand the possibilities of that process, so in a sense it's getting ahead of us. We're now using those programs to make much faster computers so that we will be able to run this process much faster. The process is feeding on itself. It's becoming faster. It's autocatalytic. We're analogous to the single-celled organisms when they were turning into multicellular organisms. We're the amoebas, and we can't quite figure out what the hell this thing is that we're creating. We're right at that point of transition, and there's something coming along after us.”* (*The Third Culture*)

AI agents are software capable of perceiving *inputs* from the digital environment, elaborating information, making decisions, and acting to achieve complex goals. They behave like flesh-and-blood operators who manage one or more processes, analyzing data and, in some cases, contributing to the design of systems or infrastructures. The Communist Left, in the “Prospetto introduttivo alla questione agraria”[^3] contained in *[Mai la merce sfamerà l'uomo](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/storici/maimerce.htm)*[^4] (1953), considered the possibility that *“a machine of the machine will replace the man on the knobs, after having recorded with electronic processes the man’s effective behaviour, the trick that distinguishes him, in order to relay it identically”*.

The 6 June edition of The *Economist* is titled “The rise of Gen-Z socialism” and [describes](https://www.economist.com/leaders/2026/06/04/how-to-fight-back-against-gen-z-socialism) a generation grappling with high inflation, unsustainable rents, and new technologies that are disrupting the labour market. This generation builds websites with AI and mobilises globally through platforms like Discord, developing a socialism 2.0, born and grown in the era of autonomous agents and mass unemployment. The fact that the weekly British periodical calls upon liberals worldwide to rally against the danger represented by the "new" socialism is significant. It is inevitable that, in the coming years, [synchronicities
](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/04/sincronia.htm) on certain themes will occur, reformist illusions will be abandoned, and a *general intellect* adequate to the situation will be formed.
