The Tuesday evening online meeting started with a comment on two fundamental texts for the understanding of the development of Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems: The “[Fragment on Machines](https://www.antiper.org/archive/materiali/marx-frammento-qr.pdf)” of Marx’s *Grundrisse* and the *thread of time* “[The Spirit of Horsepower](https://www.marxists.org/archive/bordiga/works/1953/horsepower.htm)”.

The paradigm shift introduced by machines is analyzed in the “Fragment on Machines”: if, in the manufacture, the skilled worker still employed an implement of labour to modify semi-finished products, then in modern industry, they have become the supervisor of a whole production process. Knowledge doesn’t belong to the individual worker anymore, but is instead incorporated into the means of production, socialised like never before, and has even left the walls of the factory.

Capital did not develop science, technique, and machinery to lighten the workers’ load, but did it instead to increase the productivity of labour and increase the amount of produced commodities. The development of fixed capital, in the form of science, ensures that human labour ceases to be the main factor of production (while remaining essential in the production of surplus value), because it is the general automaton that has become decisive. During this process, capitalism at the same time produces the material conditions for its own overcoming.

The machine system, having become “intelligent”, could immediately allow a drastic reduction of working hours, letting humans dedicate themselves to arts, sciences or to [a wide variety of activities](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/12/abolizione_mestieri.htm), without being crushed by a “meaningless life”[^1]. The “Fragment on Machines” constantly switches the topic of investigation between the machine's function as subjugator of the factory worker and its potential as a factor of liberation. The revolution is this whole process, including its catastrophic course.

In the article “The Spirit of Horsepower” is highlighted the passage from the artisan's tool to the machine and, lastly, to the gigantic automaton that constitutes the modern factory system.

Dario Amodel, CEO of Anthropic, in the text “[The urgency of interpretability](https://darioamodei.com/post/the-urgency-of-interpretability)" tackles the topic of the necessity to understand the “internal” processes of AI models: the new “mind” wasn’t designed by someone and it’s not possible to comprehend its whole functioning focusing on the single neuron or the single *token* (the smallest unit in which the text is decomposed to be elaborated by the model). The AI’s “magnetic resonance imaging” is a metaphor used by Amodei to point at interpretability techniques that allow to observe what happens inside the models. It concerns algorithms that analyse neuron activations and decompose them into interpretable *features*, which is to say, combinations of neurons corresponding to clearer and more understandable concepts to humans, through a signal elaboration technique known as *sparse autoencoders*. The internal configurations depend on the received *inputs* and by the training, but also by the representations that the system elaborates during its operation. In this process, the machine recursively teaches at very high speed, especially if compared to the pace of [human leaning](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/38/fare_dire_pensare_sapere.htm).

In this case, too, the bourgeoisie shows the limits of its theory of knowledge: it tends to isolate concepts instead of contextualising and reassembling them into a single set. Even when faced with the evidence of facts, current society isn’t capable of eliminating the [dualism of knowledge](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/16/critica03.htm) (man/machine, man/nature, thought/matter) and this is due to the persistence of the social division of labour.

In the report "[When AI builds itself](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement)", published on Anthropic’s website, it is stated that, brought to its extreme consequences and with sufficient computing resources, the ongoing tendency shows an AI system capable of designing and developing in a completely autonomous way its own successor. This phenomenon is called *recursive self-improvement* and resembles the *bootstrap* effect, that is, the capability of a system to launch and sustain a growth process exclusively using its own internal resources. We aren’t at this point yet – the author says – and recursive self-improvement isn’t inevitable, but it could arrive sooner than we expect.

In the essay *What Technology Wants* (2010) Kevin Kelly, co-founder of the magazine *Wired*, describing the overlaps between the world of the “born” and that of the “produced”, states:

> *“If DNA could be made into a working computer, and a working computer could be made to evolve like DNA, then there might be, or must be, a certain equivalency between the made and the born. Technology and life must share some fundamental essence.”*

*Autopoiesis* (H. Maturana and F. Varela) applies to nature as well as [machines](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/40/verso_la_singolarita_storica.htm), which are part of it. Starting from the first *chatbots*, we ha’ve now reached autonomous agents, capable of writing code alone and delegating hours of labour to other digital agents.

> *“The evidence suggests that the human role is narrowing at each step in the AI development process. Once human- and AI-authored code quality reach parity, humans will stop writing code entirely, and shift to only reviewing it. But if they can’t review code as quickly as Claude can generate it, human review will become the bottleneck to AI development.”* (“When AI builds itself”)

Anthropic researchers thus hope for a slowdown in the development of this technology, provided that it’s verifiable and shared, and the creatingon of a mechanism for global coordination, without which firms and governments would be subjected to competitive and geopolitical pressures. There is, albeit, a snag that the article does not talk about, namely that under capitalism, dominated by national interests, it is hard for the race to supremacy in the field of AI to stop. China, for example, is engaging in a ruthless [competition](https://www.ilsole24ore.com/art/ai-cina-cambia-passo-non-piu-modelli-ma-piattaforme-l-economia-reale-AIuRJgpC) with American hi-tech firms by making its own system open source (DeepSeek); a free and accessible model enables different African and South-East Asian nations to join the Chinese technological eco-system, building a vast area of influence.

In a text such as “When AI builds itself” by Anthropic a revolution is described, but it’s understandable that the authors don’t fully grasp the scope of the social consequences of technological advancement. What is this AI if not a component of the *general intellect*? In the last couple of years we’ve produced various works on the theme of the “[dissolution](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/36/necessarie_dissoluzioni.htm )” of the current mode of production (which Marx talks about in the *Grundrisse*), and AI represents an acceleration factor.

There is a social, physical and material background that lays the conditions for the “[reversal of praxis](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/19/rovesciamento_prassi.htm)”. The complexity of nature forces us to go from the study of the *object* to the study of the *process*: the machine system has its own evolutionary history, and we couldn’t be speaking of AI if industry hadn’t produced it (an enormous quantity of data to decode that requires cables, microchips, etc.). For work or out of simple curiosity, it has by now become common to interface with AI systems. The elements that make up this system remain always the human, the machine and the information that allows them to communicate, but what gets transformed is the frame of reference.

If, for the defenders of the current social form, AI is just another form of progress, for us it's a disruptive element both from the point of view of the inexorable pulverisation of millions of jobs, and from the point of view of the cognitive paradigm change (or revolution) which humanity [needs](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/38/dalla_necessita_alla_liberta.htm). Today, the species is unable to govern that which it has produced, and the limits are clearly being shown of a bourgeoisie that, undeterred, keeps  producing forces that it cannot control.

What is crucial is Marx's point regarding the passage from the formal subsumption to the real subsumption of labour under capital. In the first phase, workers are expropriated of the means of production and concentrated en masse into a separate workplace, which increases the productivity of labour. In this phase, characteristics of the previous one (artisan, professionalism) persist, but modern industry, the machine systems, emerge from its dissolution: capital, in this way, creates a world in its own image and likeness. Finding limits within the workday (absolute surplus value), capital intensifies exploitation (relative surplus value), precisely thanks to the machines.

Was a worker from the early 20th century more exploited than one from today? In 2026 the part of labour necessary to the reproduction of labour power has been reduced to a bare minimum, while the part of surplus value that is accumulated by the capitalist (who, in the meantime, became an investment fund) has grown enormously. Despite average daily working hours having been reduced, what matters is the intensification of exploitation, the relation between necessary labour and [surplus value](https://www.quinterna.org/pubblicazioni/rivista/36/macchine_automatiche_plusvalore.htm).

But even here capital encounters limitations. Robots and AIs demonstrate how the machine systems become autopoietic, and humans become the spectators of a process that they have triggered. In this transition between the formal subsumption and the real subsumption of labour, it is true that capital builds a world in its own image and likeness, but it is equally true that, at the peak of this same process of domination, we are witnessing the onset of the circumstances for its dissolution. Capital does anything to increase [the productivity of labour](https://www.quinterna.org/archivio/programma-comunista/1967/13/19670727_ricapitolazione-caduta-tendenziale.htm), but right when it has brought it to maximum capacity—when only a single minute of the daily worktime remains necessary for the reproduction of labour power, and all the rest is surplus value—it encounters a limit that cannot be surpassed: if even that last minute were to vanish, and everything were performed by the automaton, capitalism would cease to exist. There is no need to reach that extreme; capitalism collapses long before then, because its parameters are skewed:

*"Forces of production and social relations – two different sides of the development of the social individual – appear to capital as mere means, and are merely means for it to produce on its limited foundation. In fact, however, they are the material conditions to blow this foundation sky-high."* ("Fragment on Machines")
